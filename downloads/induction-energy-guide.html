Induction, gas, and the heat that misses the pan

These are widely cited ranges, not a reading from one hob. Induction often puts about 85–90% of its electrical energy into the pan. Radiant or coil hobs are often nearer 70%. A gas flame is often nearer 40%, because a lot of the heat warms the kitchen.

Using induction well

  • The pan has to be magnetic. A fridge magnet is a fair test.
  • Match the pan to the ring. Overhang and tiny pans both waste the coil.
  • A lid cuts the time water spends coming to the boil.
  • Turn the ring down or off a minute early. A heavy pan keeps cooking on residual heat.
  • If you use a pacemaker, follow the distance in the hob manual.

Where the electricity comes from

Induction is only as low-carbon as the grid or tariff behind it. A renewable electricity tariff does not change the pan. It changes what generated the kilowatt-hour. A solar cooker uses sunlight directly and no grid fuel while the sky is clear. It is slow, and it is a poor tool under heavy cloud.

Retained heat

A haybox or thermal cooker finishes food that has already come to the boil, using insulation instead of more fuel. It suits soups, beans, and rice. Check that the food is cooked through and stays hot. Do not leave perishable food lukewarm for hours.
