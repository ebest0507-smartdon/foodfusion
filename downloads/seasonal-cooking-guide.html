Seasonal cooking without the myths

Food miles are one part of the footprint. How the crop was grown is another. A tomato from a heated greenhouse can use more energy than a tomato shipped from a warmer climate. Season and method both matter.

  • Buy what is growing nearby when it tastes good and the price is fair. That is the practical version of seasonal cooking.
  • Frozen vegetables are picked and frozen quickly. They are often a lower-waste choice than a fresh punnet that dies in the drawer.
  • Tinned pulses and tomatoes are useful all year. The tin is the storage, not a failure of seasonality.
  • Plan the week around one pot that becomes lunch. That cuts both energy and the food you throw away.
  • Do not treat an out-of-season ingredient as a moral event. Cook what you can get, and waste less of it.
